## Thesis:
This dashboard explores the relationship between depression prevalence and the dominant treatment methods in the United States—therapy and medication—arguing that despite increased usage and rising healthcare costs, traditional interventions have not effectively reduced national depression rates. Through the visualizations of treatment trends, regional disparities, and lifestyle factors, the project aims to prove that physical activity—while underutilized and uncompensated—is a significantly more effective and sustainable solution for mitigating depression, and should be prioritized in both public health strategies and personal interventions.

## Context:
**Who**: This dashboard seeks to address the needs of individuals experiencing symptoms of depression, as well as those who have sought or are currently undergoing treatment through therapy or medication. It also extends to those who are exploring alternative methods for managing their mental health beyond traditional treatments. Additionally, this project is for anyone looking to adopt healthier lifestyle habits that may help prevent the onset of depression, promoting overall well-being and fulfillment.

**What**: This dashboard investigates the rise of depression in the United States despite an expanding reliance on therapy and medication. As more Americans turn to conventional treatment, the data shows that depression rates have not declined—instead, they continue to climb. At the same time, lifestyle-based alternatives like physical exercise remain largely ignored in mental health policy and funding. By confronting this imbalance, the project reframes the narrative around treatment, questioning the effectiveness of what is currently prescribed and highlighting the potential of what is not.

**How**: This dashboard uses a layered visualization strategy that draws from multiple datasets covering the years 2009 to 2022. Interactive bar charts and line graphs show the steady rise in therapy and medication usage alongside national healthcare spending, while simultaneously illustrating the increase in depression prevalence. Choropleth and heatmaps highlight regional disparities between income and depression, emphasizing structural factors. A trio of regression-based scatterplots isolates key relationships, showing that increased physical inactivity strongly correlates with higher depression rates, while greater use of therapy or medication does not reliably reduce it. Visual clarity is enhanced through standardized formatting, consistent axis labeling, and the strategic removal of redundant titles to keep focus on the data itself.

## Strategies Used: 
* Built a narrative arc with tension and resolution by introducing rising mental health trends and posing exercise as a dramatically underutilized solution, establishing a clear protagonist-antagonist dynamic through data.
* Leveraged contrast strategically using color and layout to direct attention—highlighting key comparisons (e.g., cost of exercise vs. therapy/medication) to emphasize stark discrepancies​
* Sequenced visualizations for logical flow from problem identification (rising depression, therapy/medication use) to deeper insights (heatmaps and choropleths) and ultimately to the implication (exercise as a free and effective intervention)​
* Maintained a consistent visual hierarchy through repeated placement, font sizing, and simplified axis labeling to guide user expectations and orientation across each new graph​
* Reduced clutter and distraction by stripping unnecessary chart titles, legends, and redundant labels while emphasizing direct data labeling and whitespace for clarity​
* Incorporated repetition to drive memory by reiterating key trends—like the growing cost and prevalence of traditional treatment—across multiple formats: bar, line, and scatterplot​
* Used direct labeling and intuitive positioning in graphs (e.g., placing text inside bars or beside final data points) to eliminate the need for legends and reinforce understanding without cognitive overload​
* Framed each section with an audience-focused message by aligning each graph’s insight with actionable implications, such as reconsidering public health spending or re-evaluating therapy effectiveness​
